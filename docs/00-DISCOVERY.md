## 2. Reference analysis (the four recordings)

### 2.1 What each reference does

**R1 — "Ascend" (00-07-25).** Monochrome. One sculptural object — a ribbed, fluted arch —
carries the entire page. The camera orbits and then travels *through* it: hero (arch below
the headline) → coil rising on the right beside body copy → spiral on the left beside the
client wall → final shot straight down the axis of a vortex with a glossy sphere at its centre
(it reads as an eye), where the closing call to action sits. Type is an editorial serif with a
single italic word per line for emphasis. Micro UI (tiny centred nav, small pill buttons,
10 px uppercase eyebrows) against a monumental object. Client logos sit on dark tiles at
different depths, not on a flat logo strip. Raking key light over the fins does most of the
design work. Foreground blur gives depth.

**R2 — "FIND" (00-08-01).** A building floating on clouds at golden hour. Scrolling scrambles
the headline, lifts the building, masks the wordmark with the building's image (type as a
window), then the camera passes *through* the cloud layer and descends to the city. After that
the site becomes editorial: a scroll-linked text highlight ("This isn't just about real
estate."), image-filled chevrons, a light-to-dark chapter change, a huge typographic services
list (Buy / Sell / Rent) with images sliding under each word, and a footer with an oversized
cropped wordmark.

**R3 — "Creative Digital Experiences" (00-09-18).** A dark 3D space where project cards
(glassy rounded screens) are placed along a path, overgrown with plants and flowers; the
camera glides from card to card. Glitch and pixel dissolves between projects. A left rail
asks "What are you looking for?" (Websites / Installations / XR / Multiplayer / Games) with an
"Ask me anything" input — an AI prompt used as navigation. Ends on a gold orbital sculpture
and a cinematic figure in a forest.

**R0 — ALCHE (ScreenRecording_0).** A wireframe triangle drawn in a perspective grid as the
loader; the brand mark then becomes architecture. Giant wordmark revealed through stripe masks
and lit by violet volumetric light. Chapter words ("WORK", "SERVICES") as huge 3D type. Work
presented on curved screens in 3D space. A hard inversion to a white chapter with small
Japanese type and one iridescent line. Precise instrument-like micro details (tiny labels,
crosshairs, grids).

### 2.2 Principles extracted (what VORA should learn, not copy)

1. **One continuous world; scroll is the camera.** Every strong reference keeps a single
   environment alive across the page and treats sections as shots. Transitions are camera
   moves, not fades between unrelated blocks.
2. **Light is the protagonist.** Raking light on sculptural form (R1), volumetric light (R0),
   golden hour (R2). The environment is lit like a film set, not decorated.
3. **Scale contrast with restraint.** Monumental image + tiny precise UI + one confident
   headline. Very few words per shot. Negative space is the luxury signal.
4. **One typographic gesture per shot.** Italic emphasis (R1), type-as-window (R2), chapter
   words (R0). Never several effects at once.
5. **Content as objects placed in space.** Clients and projects are placed in depth (R1 tiles,
   R3 cards, R0 curved screens) — but always along the camera path, never randomly floating.
6. **Nature meeting precision.** Plants around hard-edged screens (R3), clouds around
   architecture (R2). This is directly VORA's brief: digital architecture + natural material.
7. **Chapter inversions create rhythm.** Light→dark (R2), dark→white (R0).
8. **The climax is staged.** The call to action lands at the most dramatic moment of the
   environment (R1's vortex/eye), not in a generic footer band.
9. **Precision details signal craft.** Coordinates, indices, hairlines, crosshairs (R0).
10. **AI as a way in, not a widget.** R3's "Ask me anything" sits in the navigation rail.

### 2.3 What VORA must not take from them

- R1's monochrome ribbed object and its "Powering the *next* generation" type rhythm.
- ALCHE's triangle, wireframe-grid loader and violet volumetric palette.
- R3's pixel/dot-matrix display type, neon-purple space and particle flora.
- FIND's clouds-to-city descent as a literal sequence, and its giant cropped footer wordmark.
- Anything web3, gaming, crypto, neon or cyberpunk in tone (brief §03).

environment (water, stone, mist, vegetation) very well — confirmation needed (see

case; the vector master (SVG) is needed.
